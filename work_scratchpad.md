It seems like, because networks are more neutral, adaptable, organic, we can't be sure what it will produce ("whatever wants to happen will happen").

If we are going the low control, network route, we should be ready to celebrate all the good things that happen, rather than just evangelism. (And our success metrics should reflect that posture)

### PMO-related
    
- If you think about taking on a product development project, or an event to plan, or digital infrastructure project, what would you feel confident about, what would you feel iffy about, where would you start?

- Our job is to help teams work together more effectively. It's not especially complicated, but it requires good organization and communication.
    - We need to learn what needs to be done (and when), document it in a way that is visible to all who need to see it, and make sure everyone understands what they need to do by when... and maybe provide gentle reminders to help people hit their deadlines.
    - Honestly, plenty of projects get done without a PM - other team members just collectively do the things that a PM would do. But the hope is that having a PM devoted to organization and communication will result in fewer balls being dropped, deadlines being missed, less miscommunication, and less anxiety on the part of all the other team members. We should make work easier and more enjoyable for everyone else on the project.
    - That's kind of our baseline. Sometimes we have opportunities to add value to projects in other ways, especially if we've built good working relationships over time with teammates. But it'll usually still be through suggestion, not decision-making authority. "I know this is not my lane, but here are my two cents:"





PM role:

Baseline: Document a detailed project plan in a way that is visible to all who need to see it, and then keep everyone accountable to execute the plan.

Above and Beyond: Anticipate issues/risks, find places where the plan can be streamlined, remove obstacles standing in the way of teammates doing their part, learn and do parts of the project that need to be done but don't fall to any other teammates, catch misunderstandings and push for clarity
