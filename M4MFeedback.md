Based on the provided documents, the Made for Mission conference was viewed as a successful "proof of concept". While the event received high praise for its content and collaborative spirit, the feedback also highlighted significant logistical and structural challenges.

## **What Went Well ("Goods")**

### **Content and Vision**

**Keynote Speakers and Worship:** Attendees and staff highly praised the keynote speakers and the worship band.

**Proof of Concept:** Leadership felt the event successfully demonstrated that a family-focused mission conference is a viable and needed model.

**Subject Matter:** The overall subject matter and the integration of kids' programming with adult content were seen as tremendous successes.

### **Engagement and Collaboration**

**Ministry Fair:** This was a major highlight, exuding high energy and facilitating significant conversations . Participants appreciated the large-scale involvement of both staff and external "guides".

**Networking and Connections:** On-site meals and specific pathway tables (e.g., blended families, disability) allowed for "significant connections" that might not have happened otherwise.

**Collaborative Spirit:** Despite system friction, the team’s ability to collaborate across different organizational departments (Core and Expansion) was noted as a strength.

### **Operations and Logistics**

**Budget Management:** Despite needing a $40,000 infusion, the event ended "in the black" by approximately $3,700, landing very close to its projected budget.

**Prayer Support:** The release of weekly prayer requests was seen as a positive, adaptive spiritual foundation for the event.

---

## **Areas for Improvement ("Grows")**

### **Organizational Structure**

**Role Clarity:** There was significant confusion regarding who was in charge of what (e.g., signage responsibilities), leading to frustration for those "in the middle" .

**Leadership Presence:** Some staff felt a "pull back" from top leadership as the event approached and desired more consistent rallying cries and direction .

**Sustainable Staffing:** Leadership noted that "moonlighting" to run the conference is unsustainable; future iterations require a dedicated, full-time "Made for Mission" team.


### **Logistics and Scheduling**

**Programming Overload:** The schedule was described as too dense, with too many speakers (five) and workshops (eight), which diluted the impact of the content.

**Venue Navigation:** The "maze-like" layout of the Concord Church facility made finding seminars difficult for participants.

**Timing:** The first week of December was cited as a stressful and busy time for families and staff to travel.

### **Marketing and Attendance**

**Target Audience Ratio:** The attendance was roughly 65% staff and 35% non-staff . To reach "guides" and external influencers more effectively, leadership suggested removing "limited bandwidth" marketing constraints used for this pilot .

**Barriers to Families:** Costs and travel remain hurdles for non-staff families. Suggestions included moving the event to the summer or using a "conference in a box" model to localize the experience .


### **On-Site Experience**

**Food Service:** While the food was good, there were logistical backups in lines. Kimberly suggested having two lines instead of one for future dinners.

**Atmosphere:** Some participants found the networking environments too loud to hold comfortable conversations.

**Production Quality:** Brian Winkler noted that the "shoestring" budget was evident in limited video capture, lack of B-roll for future marketing, and minimal on-site branding .
