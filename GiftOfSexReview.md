#### Preface

- I appreciate the generosity with which they talk about their upbringing, even though they acknowledge that it left them ill-prepared for engaging sexually within marriage.
- Their observations seem to line up very closely with the gender differences page in the guidebook. However, they seem to do less qualifying of their language. In the guidebook, men/women *tend* to be this way, but the book just says that they *are* this way. At a WTR event, it may be good for speakers who want to mention this book to preface it by saying, this could be a helpful book if husband and wife both lean in the direction of the most common gender tendencies. (individual difference - and deviation from gendered tendencies - is acknowledged on p6)

#### Ch3

#### Ch4

I appreciate the recognition that Ephesians 5:21 is the context for what follows, and not the end of a previous section.

"We look to love as our criterion because the New Testament gives no teaching on how to enjoy each other sexually." - I like the simplicity of this, and also how it refocuses us on the center of the Christian ethic. Sexuality is not some special category with a laundry list of guidelines. It, like the rest of the Christian life, is meant to be an expression of love and looking to the interests of the other.
