      </li>
      <li id="Gl">
        <p><strong>I. Created in the Image of God with Dignity and Worth</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li id="45">
            <p>We believe every person is uniquely made in the image of God (Gen 1:27; 9:6; Jas 3:9). As image bearers, human beings of every ____ possess God-given dignity, worth and honor.</p>
          </li>
          <li id="wq">
            <p>As image bearers, Adam and Eve were given a mandate to “be fruitful and multiply,” to “fill the earth and subdue it” and to “have dominion” over all living things (Gen 1:28–30). This design is lived out in four relationships that reflect the goodness of God’s creation:</p>
            <ul>
              <li id="oO">
                <p><span>Relationship with God</span><span>: A personal relationship with God (Gen 2:7).</span></p>
              </li>
              <li id="bk">
                <p><span>Relationship to Self</span><span>: God-given understanding of themselves as created, God-dependent and relational beings made in the image of a relational God (Gen 1:27; 2:18–20, 24).</span></p>
              </li>
              <li id="0r">
                <p><span>Relationships with Others</span><span>: Interdependent relationships with others that include generations of families, households, tribes and nations (Gen 2:21–25; 10:1–32).</span></p>
              </li>
              <li id="HU">
                <p><span>Relationship to Creation</span><span>: Stewardship and care for the created world (Gen 2:15</span><span>).</span></p>
              </li>
            </ul>
          </li>
          <li id="RHV">
            <p/>
          </li>
          <li id="pCO">
            <p>While people do bear God’s image as individuals, they bear his image in an even fuller way in community. This is because God is a community in and of himself (three persons, one God), and there are aspects of God that an individual cannot image alone (like mutual love).</p>
          </li>
          <li id="xoy">
            <p>The family is the fundamental community that God has given us in which to bear his image together. The family is part of God’s good creation from the beginning of time. He created people, male and female (Gen 1:27), and having shown that it isn’t good for man to be alone (Gen 2:18), brought Adam and Eve together into a life-long covenant (Matt. 19:4-6), blessing them and commanding them to have children and fill the earth (Gen 1:28). </p>
          </li>
          <li id="ShR">
            <p>Part of the reason families have such great capacity to bear God’s image is because he has designed families to contain inherent diversity: diversity of gender, diversity of age, diversity of roles, and potentially diversity of much more. As each member of a family bears God’s image in their unique circumstances and callings, together they paint a fuller, deeper picture of who God is. And we should expect this to look different from family to family because of how differently families may be composed, whether single-parent families, blended families, married couples without kids, unmarried but committed couples with or without kids, families with adopted and foster children, households with multiple generations and/or extended family living under one roof, etc. We need look no further than Scripture to see how complex (and sometimes messy) family composition can be.</p>
          </li>

      <li id="ny">
        <p><strong>II. Living in a Fallen World</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li id="kY">
            <p>God’s good creation was corrupted by the rebellion of our first parents, Adam and Eve (Gen 3:1–24). The Fall impacted four key relationships:</p>
            <ul>
              <li id="mW">
                <p><span>Relationship with God</span><span>: Every human being is born alienated from God and stands under God’s righteous judgment (Rom 3:23; 5:10; 6:23; Eph 2:3). Alienation from God can be expressed individually (Ps 14:1; Rom 3:23) and corporately (Exod 32:1–6).</span></p>
              </li>
              <li id="Jm">
                <p><span>Relationship to Self</span><span>: Corruption extends to every aspect of our human constitution — body, mind and soul (Gen 2:17; 6:5; Rom 5:12–18; Eph 2:1–3; 1 Pet 2:11).</span></p>
              </li>
              <li id="jU">
                <p><span>Relationships with Others</span><span>: Human beings are alienated from each other (Eph 2:11–14; Rom 1:29–31; Gal 5:16-21). Generational alienation exists not only between individuals but also between families, ethnic groups and nations.</span></p>
              </li>
              <li id="3C">
                <p><span>Relationship to Creation</span><span>: In his judgment on sin, God warned our first parents they would experience futility and difficulty in their work: “... Cursed is the ground because of you; through painful toil you will eat food from it all the days of your life” (Gen 3:17).</span></p>
              </li>
            </ul>
          </li>
          <li id="Onn">
            <p>One thing that all families have in common is that they are comprised of people who were raised and formed in imperfect families, and who bring the baggage of those imperfections into their new family. This can result in any number of unhealthy ways of relating to one another that fail to accord with God’s calling on various members of a family. In some cases, these unhealthy patterns lead to the division of a family. While this is not ideal, sometimes unhealthy patterns reach a level of severity such that division is necessary in order to ensure the protection of the family’s members.</p>
          </li>
          <li id="uMNC">
            <p>Ultimately, a growing relationship with Jesus Christ is essential for healthy marriage, parenting and family relationships, and where the Holy Spirit is at work there can be hope of redemption.</p>
          </li>

      <li id="U3">
        <p><strong>III. Redemption, Reconciliation and a New Community</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li id="1a">
            <p>“For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son …” (John 3:16). Jesus counter-culturally modeled care and value for those from every generation (e.g., welcoming children over the protests of his disciples in Matthew 19:14). Through his incarnation, teachings, obedience, miracles, crucifixion, resurrection and enthronement, Jesus Christ reconciled men and women to God (2 Cor 5:18–19); granted eternal life (John 3:16; 17:3); brought freedom and liberation from bondage to sin (including patterns of generational brokenness), Satan and evil (Luke 4:18-19; Rom 6:1-11; 1 John 3:8); inaugurated God’s kingly rule (Matt 4:17; 10:7; 12:28; Rom 14:17); and will eventually return and restore the world he created (Rom 8:18–25; 1 Cor 15:25–28; Rev 21:1–14). Christ’s redemptive work transforms all four relationships: God, self, others and creation.</p>
          </li>
          <li id="fsBg">
            <p/>
          </li>
          <li id="pnH">
            <p>The hope of the gospel as it relates to the redemption of the family takes two primary forms:</p>
            <ul>
              <li id="fRIf">
                <p><strong>The hope of redemption within the here-and-now family:</strong><span> Through the empowering work of the Holy Spirit, we can be the people God calls us to be for our families (and they can be the people God calls them to be for us). With God’s help, our families can be places of love and emotional support (Ps 103:13; Isa 66:13; Eph 5:25; 1 Thess 2:7), economic provision (Num 36:7-9; Lk 11:11; Jn 19:25-27; 1 Tim 5:8), guidance</span><s>—</s><span>spiritual and otherwise (Deut 6:7; Prov 22:6; 1 Thess 2:11-12; Heb 12:7), protection (Matt 2:13-14; Lk 13:34), and honor (Ex 20:12; Lev 19:3).</span></p>
              </li>
              <li id="4Kqw">
                <p><strong>The hope of inclusion in an enduring spiritual family:</strong><span> Those who belong to God belong to his family. This is an incredible promise, both for today and for eternity. God calls his family to love one another (John 13:34), be devoted to and honor one another (Rom 12:10), live in harmony with one another (Rom 12:16), accept one another (Rom 15:7), encourage and build one another up (1 Thess 5:11), care for one another (1 Cor 12:25), serve one another (Gal 5:13), bear one another’s burdens (Gal 6:2), be patient with one another (Col 3:13), speak the truth in love to one another (Eph 4:15), be kind and compassionate to one another (Eph 4:32), submit to one another (1 Peter 5:5), consider one another’s interests (Phil 2:4), teach one another (Col 3:16), comfort one another (1 Thess 4:18), show hospitality to one another (1 Peter 4:9), act with humility toward one another (1 Peter 5:5), and pray for one another (James 5:16), and when we mess these things up, we confess our faults to one another (James 5:16) and forgive one another (Eph 4:2). </span><em>That</em><span> is the kind of family and community that we all want and need.</span></p>
              </li>

      <li id="aQ">
        <p><strong>IV. A Redeemed Community on Mission to the Nations</strong></p>
        <ul>
          <li id="tL">
            <p>Our God is a missionary God (John 20:21–23). God’s mission to redeem the nations began with his call to Abraham to mediate God’s blessing to all nations (Gen 12:1–3), continued through Israel’s role as a light to the nations (Exod 19:5–6; Deut 4:5-8) and climaxed when God the Father sent Jesus Christ to bless all nations through his death and resurrection.</p>
          </li>
          <li id="HE">
            <p>Before ascending his throne, Jesus sent his disciples to be his witnesses, just as the Father sent him (John 20:21–22). Jesus promised God’s Spirit would empower them as they went (Matt 28:18–20; Mark 16:14–16; Luke 24:46–49; Acts 1:8). Following the ascension, the mighty outpouring of God’s Spirit at Pentecost unleashed the spread of the good news of the gospel from Jerusalem to Judea and Samaria, the Roman Empire and — over the course of two millennia — into our lives today.</p>
          </li>
          <li id="Hydg">
            <p/>
          </li>
          <li id="NpX">
            <p><span>God invites his people to participate with him in this mission to bring the hope of the gospel to the nations and redemption to the world—and he invites us to participate </span><em>as a family</em><span>. We see this in the book of Acts (2:42-47):</span></p>
            <ul>
              <li id="Zgis">
                <p>“And they devoted themselves to the apostles’ teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers. And awe came upon every soul, and many wonders and signs were being done through the apostles. And all who believed were together and had all things in common. And they were selling their possessions and belongings and distributing the proceeds to all, as any had need. And day by day, attending the temple together and breaking bread in their homes, they received their food with glad and generous hearts, praising God and having favor with all the people. And the Lord added to their number day by day those who were being saved.”</p>
              </li>
            </ul>
          </li>
          <li id="TWtU">
            <p>By God’s grace, we are to follow in their footsteps. Together, we live out the one anothers, creating a compelling community that gives the world a glimpse of the wonderful God we serve; together, we share the hope of the gospel and invite others to be reconciled to God and join his family; together, we seek to bless all people, in anticipation of the day when God’s family will include all tribes, tongues and nations.</p>
